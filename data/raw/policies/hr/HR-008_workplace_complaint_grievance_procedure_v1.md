# Workplace Complaint and Grievance Procedure

## 1. Purpose
NexaCore Solutions Pvt. Ltd. is committed to maintaining a professional, safe, and equitable work environment where every employee feels valued and heard. We recognize that in any high-pressure professional setting, conflicts and grievances may arise. The purpose of this procedure is to provide a fair, transparent, and impartial mechanism for raising concerns, ensuring they are investigated thoroughly and resolved justly.

## 2. Scope
This procedure applies to all employees, contractors, and third-party vendors working within NexaCore facilities or interacting with NexaCore staff. It covers a wide range of workplace complaints, including but not limited to:
- Disputes regarding management style or interpersonal conflicts.
- Disagreements over pay, benefits, or the application of the **Leave Policy (HR-002)**.
- Allegations of discrimination, bullying, or harassment (as prohibited in the **Code of Conduct (HR-006)**).
- Health and safety concerns within the office.
- Alleged breaches of company policy or ethical misconduct.

## 3. Defining a Workplace Complaint
A workplace complaint is defined as any formal or informal expression of dissatisfaction regarding a professional situation that an employee believes is unfair, improper, or in violation of company policy.
- **Informal Grievances:** Minor disagreements or misunderstandings that can be resolved through a simple conversation.
- **Formal Grievances:** Serious issues that require a documented investigation, such as harassment, fraud, or systemic unfairness.

## 4. Reporting Channels and Process

### 4.1 Informal Resolution (The First Step)
Whenever possible, employees are encouraged to resolve minor conflicts informally. This is often the fastest and most effective way to maintain a positive working relationship.
- **Direct Dialogue:** The employee is encouraged to speak directly with the person involved to clarify misunderstandings.
- **Managerial Facilitation:** If a direct approach is uncomfortable, the employee may ask their **Reporting Manager** to facilitate a professional discussion.

### 4.2 Formal Reporting (The Official Step)
If an informal approach is unsuccessful, or if the nature of the complaint is too severe for an informal resolution (e.g., allegations of sexual harassment, fraud, or severe discrimination), a formal complaint must be filed.
- **Reporting to Manager:** The employee submits a formal report via the HR portal, addressed to their Reporting Manager.
- **Reporting to HR:** If the complaint involves the Reporting Manager, or if the employee feels uncomfortable reporting to them, the report should be filed directly with the HR department.
- **Anonymous Reporting (Whistleblower):** For reports involving senior leadership or systemic corporate misconduct, employees may use the company's secure, anonymous whistleblower portal. This ensures that the reporter is protected from any identification.

## 5. The Investigation Process

### 5.1 Acknowledgement and Initial Assessment
Upon receipt of a formal complaint, the HR department will acknowledge the report within 3 business days.
- **Initial Assessment:** HR will determine if the complaint falls under this procedure or if it should be routed to another department (e.g., a technical issue to IT).
- **Assignment:** A neutral investigator from the HR department, or an external third party if the case is high-profile, will be assigned to the case.

### 5.2 Fact-Finding and Evidence Gathering
The investigation is conducted with a commitment to impartiality and thoroughness.
- **Interviews:** The investigator will conduct private interviews with the complainant, the respondent, and any relevant witnesses.
- **Digital Evidence:** The investigator will review relevant emails, Slack/Teams logs, project records, and other digital artifacts.
- **Strict Confidentiality:** All parties involved—including the investigator—are required to maintain strict confidentiality. Unauthorized disclosure of the investigation's details is a violation of the **Code of Conduct (HR-006)**.

### 5.3 Right to a Fair Hearing
The respondent (the person the complaint is against) will be formally notified of the allegations. They will be given a fair opportunity to respond to the claims, provide their own evidence, and call their own witnesses.

## 6. Determination and Outcomes

### 6.1 The Decision
Based on the evidence gathered, the investigator (or a review panel for complex cases) will reach a determination:
- **Substantiated:** The evidence supports the claim that a violation of policy or conduct occurred.
- **Unsubstantiated:** The evidence does not support the claim, or it is found that no violation occurred.
- **Inconclusive:** There is insufficient evidence to make a definitive determination.

### 6.2 Resolution and Corrective Action
If a complaint is substantiated, NexaCore will take appropriate and proportional action. This may include:
- **Mediation:** Mandatory professional mediation to resolve an interpersonal conflict.
- **Training:** Required behavioral or sensitivity training.
- **Disciplinary Action:** This can range from a written warning to demotion or immediate termination of employment.
- **Systemic Change:** Updating a company policy or process to prevent the issue from recurring.

### 6.3 Notification of Outcome
Both the complainant and the respondent will be notified of the final decision in writing. While the specific disciplinary action taken against another employee may be kept confidential, the complainant will be informed whether the complaint was substantiated and that a resolution has been reached.

## 7. Escalation and the Appeals Process

### 7.1 Request for Review
If either party believes the investigation was flawed or the outcome was unfair, they may request a formal review within 10 business days of the notification.
- **Basis for Appeal:** The appeal must be based on the emergence of new evidence or a demonstrable failure in the investigation process (e.g., a witness was ignored).
- **The Appeal Panel:** Appeals are reviewed by a panel consisting of the Head of HR and a senior leader from a different department to ensure an unbiased perspective.

## 8. Non-Retaliation Guarantee
NexaCore strictly prohibits any form of retaliation against an employee who raises a complaint or participates in an investigation in good faith.
- **Definition of Retaliation:** This includes but is not limited to: demotion, exclusion from projects, sudden negative performance reviews, social isolation, or hostile behavior.
- **Consequences:** Any employee found to be retaliating against a complainant or witness will face severe disciplinary action, up to and including immediate termination.

## 9. Documentation and Record Retention
All records related to workplace grievances—including the original report, interview notes, evidence, and the final determination—are stored securely by the HR department.
- **Retention Period:** Records are kept for the duration of the employee's tenure plus five years, in accordance with local legal requirements.
- **Access:** Access to these files is strictly limited to the Head of HR and the assigned investigator.
