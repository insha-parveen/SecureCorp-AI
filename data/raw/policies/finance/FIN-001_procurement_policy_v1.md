# Procurement Policy

## 1. Introduction and Purpose
The purpose of this policy is to establish a rigorous, transparent, and accountable framework for the procurement of all goods and services required by NexaCore Solutions Pvt. Ltd. In a high-growth technology consulting environment, the ability to acquire the right tools, infrastructure, and professional services is critical to maintaining our competitive edge and ensuring the success of our client deliveries.

This policy is designed to ensure that NexaCore achieves the best overall value—balancing quality, cost, reliability, and sustainability—while mitigating financial and operational risks. By centralizing procurement controls and requiring formal authorization, we ensure that corporate funds are spent judiciously and in alignment with our strategic objectives.

## 2. Scope and Application
This policy applies to all employees, contractors, and consultants acting on behalf of NexaCore Solutions across all global operating hubs, including Lucknow (LKO), Bengaluru (BLR), Dubai (DXB), and Singapore (SIN). 

The scope covers the procurement of:
- **Capital Expenditures (CapEx):** Long-term assets such as servers, office furniture, and high-end workstations.
- **Operating Expenditures (OpEx):** Recurring costs such as software licenses, cloud subscriptions, office supplies, and professional services.
- **Project-Specific Procurement:** Hardware or software specifically purchased for a client engagement, which may be subsequently billable to the client.

## 3. Core Procurement Principles

### 3.1 Value for Money (VfM)
NexaCore does not simply seek the lowest price. "Value for Money" is defined as the optimal combination of cost, quality, and risk. When evaluating proposals, the Finance department and the requesting manager must consider the total cost of ownership (TCO), which includes:
- Initial acquisition cost.
- Implementation and configuration fees.
- Maintenance and support costs over the asset's lifespan.
- Expected longevity and scalability.

### 3.2 Ethical Sourcing and Conduct
Procurement is a high-risk area for conflicts of interest. All procurement activities must be conducted with absolute integrity.
- **Adherence to Code of Conduct:** Every person involved in the procurement process must strictly follow the **Code of Conduct (HR-006)**.
- **Conflict of Interest:** Employees must disclose any personal or financial relationship with a potential vendor. No employee may participate in the selection or approval of a vendor if they have a conflict of interest.
- **Anti-Bribery:** The acceptance of gifts, kickbacks, or any form of incentive from a vendor to influence a procurement decision is strictly prohibited and is grounds for immediate termination.

### 3.3 Accountability and Auditability
Every procurement action—from the initial request to the final payment—must leave a digital audit trail. This includes the original request, quotes received, the approval chain, and the final Purchase Order.

## 4. The Procurement Lifecycle

### 4.1 Requirement Identification and the Purchase Request (PR)
The procurement process begins when a business need is identified.
1. **Need Analysis:** The requester must determine if the required item is already available within the company (e.g., in the IT asset inventory) before requesting a new purchase.
2. **The Purchase Request (PR):** The requester submits a PR via the Finance portal. The PR must include:
    - A detailed description of the item or service.
    - The quantity and requested delivery date.
    - The estimated cost based on preliminary research.
    - The specific project code or cost center to be charged.
    - A clear business justification explaining why the purchase is necessary.
3. **Budgetary Review:** The Finance department reviews the PR to ensure funds are available within the department's current fiscal budget. If the budget is exceeded, a "Budget Exception Request" must be signed by the Department Head and the CFO.

### 4.2 Vendor Sourcing and Selection Process
To ensure competitive pricing and quality, NexaCore employs a tiered sourcing strategy based on the value of the procurement.

#### 4.2.1 Low-Value Procurement (< ₹50,000)
For small, routine purchases, a streamlined process is used.
- **Preferred Vendors:** Procurement from pre-approved vendors (`VEN-NNN`) is encouraged.
- **Sourcing:** A single quote from a trusted vendor is typically sufficient for items under this threshold.

#### 4.2.2 Medium-Value Procurement (₹50,001 – ₹500,000)
For more significant purchases, a competitive bidding process is required.
- **Competitive Bidding:** At least three independent, written quotes from qualified vendors must be obtained.
- **Evaluation:** The requesting manager and a Finance representative evaluate the quotes based on price, quality, and delivery time.
- **Selection:** The best-value vendor is selected, and the justification for choosing a vendor who was not the lowest bidder must be documented.

#### 4.2.3 High-Value Procurement (> ₹500,000)
For strategic or high-cost acquisitions, a formal Request for Proposal (RFP) process is mandatory.
- **RFP Issuance:** A detailed specification document is sent to a curated list of potential vendors.
- **Proposal Evaluation:** A Procurement Committee (consisting of the Department Head, a Finance Manager, and a technical expert) reviews the proposals.
- **Due Diligence:** The committee may request vendor demonstrations, client references, and a review of the vendor's financial stability.
- **CFO Approval:** All high-value procurements require final sign-off from the CFO (Kabir Nair).

### 4.3 The Purchase Order (PO) Mechanism
The Purchase Order is the primary control mechanism for spending. No expenditure is authorized until a PO is issued.
- **Issuance:** Once a vendor is selected, the Finance department generates a PO (`PO-NNNN`).
- **Legal Status:** The PO serves as a legally binding contract. It specifies the exact scope of work, the agreed price, payment terms (e.g., Net 30), and the delivery schedule.
- **PO Modifications:** Any change to the price or scope after a PO is issued requires a "PO Amendment," which must go through the same approval process as the original request.
- **Unauthorized Purchases:** Purchases made without an approved PO are considered "Unauthorized Expenditures." The company may refuse to pay such invoices, and the employee may be held personally liable for the cost.

## 5. Procurement Controls and Compliance

### 5.1 The Three-Way Match Control
To ensure that NexaCore only pays for what it ordered and actually received, the Finance department performs a "Three-Way Match" before any payment is executed.
Payment is released only when these three documents are perfectly aligned:
1. **Purchase Order (PO):** The authorized order specifying the cost and quantity.
2. **Receiving Report/Delivery Note:** Confirmation from the employee that the goods were received or the service was completed.
3. **Vendor Invoice:** The bill sent by the vendor.

Any discrepancy in these documents triggers an automatic payment hold and a dispute investigation.

### 5.2 Vendor Management and Onboarding
To maintain quality and security, all vendors must be officially onboarded.
- **Vendor Registration:** New vendors must apply through the Finance portal, providing tax IDs and bank verification.
- **Vendor IDs:** Every approved vendor is assigned a unique ID (`VEN-NNN`).
- **Performance Review:** High-value vendors are reviewed annually by the Finance department to ensure they continue to meet our standards for quality and reliability.

### 5.3 Prohibited Procurement Behaviors
The following actions are strictly prohibited and constitute a breach of the **Code of Conduct (HR-006)**:
- **Split Purchasing:** The practice of dividing a single large purchase into multiple smaller orders to stay below an approval threshold (e.g., splitting a ₹60,000 purchase into two ₹30,000 orders to avoid Finance Manager approval).
- **Favorite Vendor Bias:** Selecting a vendor based on personal relationships rather than the "Value for Money" principle.
- **Direct Commitment:** Making a verbal or email promise to a vendor that the company will pay for a service before a PO is issued.

## 6. Special Procurement Categories

### 6.1 Software as a Service (SaaS) and Recurring Subscriptions
Due to the nature of cloud services, SaaS procurement differs from physical goods.
- **Master POs:** For recurring subscriptions, a "Master PO" may be issued for the total annual contract value. Monthly invoices are then drawn against this balance.
- **Auto-Renewal Review:** All recurring subscriptions must be reviewed 30 days before the renewal date to ensure the tool is still providing value.

### 6.2 Sole Source Justification
In rare instances where only one vendor is capable of providing the required product (e.g., a proprietary legacy software license), the competitive bidding process may be waived.
- **Justification Form:** The requester must submit a "Sole Source Justification" explaining why no other vendors are viable.
- **Approval:** This form must be approved by the Department Head and the CFO.

### 6.3 Internal Recharges
When one department provides a service to another, a nominal internal recharge may be processed for accounting purposes. These are handled as internal transfers and do not require a formal PO.
